\section{Introduction} 
\par
The IVU18 is a new in-vacuum-undulator that will be installed on two SIRIUS low-beta straight sections. Nowadays IVU18 is in the project phase and its specifications are not fully closed yet, especially regarding the roll-off ones. High roll-off values can lead to the arising of strong integrated multipole components on the straight section of the IDs and high-order multipole components can be prejudicial to SIRIUS's dynamic aperture. 
\par
The first roll-off specification proposed was $0.01\%$ at $x=6$ mm for a minimum gap of 4.3 mm. This value, however, implies large widths for the magnetic blocks (64 mm), and as a consequence, the force necessary to keep the gap opened is high (about 30 kN for the minimum gap). To reduce the magnetic force a new roll-off specification was proposed ($0.5\%$ at 6 mm). This document reports the effect of an IVU18 with such fields on the SIRIUS's dynamic aperture. 

\section{Simulations results}
\par The following figure shows the field roll-off for blocks with widths 64 mm, 31 mm, and 29 mm. These fields were calculated using the IMA group "insertion-devices" library (\url{https://github.com/lnls-ima/insertion-devices}), which makes use of the software RADIA. The subdivisions adopted for these calculations were [8, 4, 4] for the magnetic blocks and [34, 5, 5] for the poles.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{field-rolloff.png}
\caption{Field roll-off for different block widths}
\label{fig:fieldrolloff}
\end{figure}

As it's possible to see in figure \ref{fig:fieldrolloff} blocks with 31 mm width provide a field roll-off of $0.47\%$ very near the new specified value, while a 29 mm width provides a roll-off almost 2.5 bigger.

\par By comparing the effect of the insertion device for different block widths it is possible to see that the effect of a 64 mm block is almost the same as a 31 mm block or even a 29 mm block. The boundaries do not experience considerable changes, and, even though the diffusion increases with the insertion of the ID, it does not change considerably with the variation of roll-off (for the range considered).

\section{Conclusion}
\par With the results shown here, it is possible to conclude that the biggest effect on the dynamic aperture happens after the insertion of the IVU18 and does not change considerably when the widths of the blocks are reduced to the values considered here, therefore, the reduction of the roll-off specification for $0.5\%$ will not affect the dynamic aperture.
